Find your first customer for an AI project: a 4-week plan

Turn a side project into something someone pays for or at least uses weekly. A practical plan: pick a narrow user, talk to ten people, ship a small offer.

To find a first customer, pick one narrow group with a recurring pain, talk to ten of them, offer a small paid or committed pilot, and build only what that pilot needs. Skip the landing page until someone has said yes.

Week 1: pick a narrow user and pain

"Developers" is not a customer. "Solo owners of small e-commerce shops who answer the same shipping questions every day" is. Write down who they are, where they gather, what task costs them hours, and what they do about it now. The best targets are people you already know or communities you are part of, because you can reach them this week.

Week 2: talk to ten people

Ask about the last time the problem happened, what they did, what it cost, and what they tried. Do not pitch. Do not ask "would you use this", since everyone says yes. Ask if you can show a rough version next week. If three of ten lean in, you have a signal. If none do, change the target before you write more code.

Week 3: ship a pilot, not a product

Build the smallest thing that does the job for one person: a script, a shared spreadsheet plus an agent, a bot in their chat. Run it by hand if you need to. Agree on a result in advance, such as "saves two hours a week", and on what they will pay or commit to if it does. A free pilot with no commitment tells you little.

Week 4: review and decide

Measure the result against what you agreed. Did they use it twice? Would they miss it? Ask for a referral to someone with the same problem. If it worked, write down what you did so you can repeat it for customer two. If it did not, you learned in a month and not in a year. Either way, keep notes on every conversation, because the exact words customers use for their pain are the best copy for your next page and the best test cases for your next eval.

Tools for the planning part

If you have a product link and want a starting plan for who to reach, Grow at grow.flisk.si takes a link to your product and returns a plan for finding the first customers. It is in beta, and a generated plan is a draft to challenge, not an answer. Publish a short case study of the pilot (see portfolio that gets interviews), and if the idea is a cost-saving one, ground your claims in numbers from your evals.

Questions people ask

What if no one will pay?

Then you have learned what not to build. Revisit the pain: is it frequent and costly enough that someone would pay to remove it?

Should I build the whole product first?

No. Do the job by hand or with a small script for one customer first, then automate what repeats.
