The four parts

1

Make it real about 75 min

Take a real multi-service app and make it start with one flisk up. Pick one: example-voting-app (5 services, 3 languages), react-express-mongodb, or any public app with two or more services you would rather use. Copy it into your new repository (a plain copy, not a fork, and never a pull request to the original), then run npx @flisk-sh/flisk import . --dry-run to see the plan, the same without --dry-run to write it, and flisk up to start it. You need Docker running. The Flisk README has the rest.

DeliverThe converted app in your repository, with .flisk/project.json and a service.json per service. A README.md that links the original app, shows how to start it with flisk up, and lists what was hard. That friction list is the most useful part for us.

2

Find buyers about 60 min

Find three real teams, companies or open-source projects that lose time on local setup and would pay, or adopt, to stop. You choose the market and say why.

DeliverBUYERS.md: one ## heading per buyer with who they are, a public link that shows the pain (an issue, a thread, a CONTRIBUTING guide), and the first message you would send them. Write it, do not send it.

3

Pitch about 45 min

Write the one page you would send an engineering lead at a team building AI agents.

DeliverPITCH.md with a ## Why Flisk section (against Docker Compose, Tilt or devcontainers, honestly, including where they win) and a ## Pricing section: what is free, what is paid, for whom.

4

Product angle about 15 min

One feature that would make Flisk more useful for AI-heavy workflows, and how you would know it worked.

DeliverAn issue in your repository: the problem, the feature, and the signal you would watch.

Please
  • Contact no one and touch no other repository. Do not open issues, pull requests or comments on the example apps, on Flisk, or on your buyers' projects, and do not send your buyer messages. Everything stays in your own repository. A submission that contacted other projects is not reviewed.
  • Do not ping anyone from your repository either. No @mentions in your issue, and no links to other projects' issues or pull requests in your issue or commit messages: GitHub notifies those people and posts a "mentioned this" note on their issue. Link buyer pain in BUYERS.md only, where it notifies no one.
  • Keep the original app's LICENSE file next to the copied code.
  • No personal data. In BUYERS.md name teams and companies, not private people, and write no email addresses or phone numbers. Your repository is public.
  • Keep it honest: write what really happened, including what did not work.
  • Do not commit .env, node_modules or generated Flisk files.

How you are evaluated

The self-check below only confirms that all four parts are there. The founder then reads every complete submission and scores four things from 1 to 5:

  1. Technical depth. Did the app really run? Do you understand why the hard parts were hard? Part 1
  2. Customer insight. Are these real buyers with real, linked pain, and would your first message get a reply? Part 2
  3. Clarity. Would an engineering lead understand the value in one minute, and believe it? Part 3
  4. Ownership. Does the product idea come from what you saw, with a way to measure it? Part 4

Strong submissions are invited to a 30-minute call: a short live sales conversation with the founder. The self-check code and its tests are open: check.js, check.test.js.
