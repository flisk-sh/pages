CI Queue Time vs Run Time vs Cost: What to Measure First

Separate waiting for a runner from running, and billed minutes from wall-clock time, so you optimize the right thing: speed, concurrency or cost.

Three numbers get mixed up: queue time (waiting for a runner), run time (executing), and billed minutes (summed across all jobs). They need different fixes. Measure each one separately before you decide what to optimize.

Queue time

Queue time is from when a run or job is created until it starts. For a run: startedAt minus createdAt in the gh run list fields. If queue time is high, steps are not your problem. Typical causes: too many concurrent jobs for your plan or your self-hosted pool, big matrices, or many superseded runs. Cancelling stale runs often helps most, see concurrency groups.

Run time (wall clock)

Run time is how long a developer waits once the run starts. Parallel jobs reduce it. The longest chain of dependent jobs decides it. Optimize this for developer feedback speed, with caching and sharding, as in matrix builds.

Billed minutes

Billed minutes are the sum of all job durations, not the wall-clock time. A matrix of 8 shards at 5 minutes each is 40 billed minutes but 5 minutes of waiting. Billing also depends on runner type and plan, and rules change, so check GitHub's current billing page rather than assuming rates.

Caching, cancelling and skipping are the rare changes that help everything at once.

Where to read queue time

For a run, take startedAt minus createdAt. For a job, the jobs endpoint gives created_at and started_at, so the same subtraction works. Plot the median weekly. A rising queue time at the same run volume means your concurrency ceiling is closer than before. A spike at a certain hour usually means a scheduled job or a burst of pushes after a meeting. Moving schedules off the busy hour is a free fix.

Pick one goal

Decide whether you are optimizing feedback speed, concurrency or cost, since pushing all three at once leads to muddled changes. Then measure that number before and after, as in measuring speedups. For a view of median and p90 run time across an org, see Cilytics, which does not report billed minutes or queue time.

Document the choice in your contributing guide: what you optimize, how you measure it and who reviews the number. A shared goal prevents the situation where one person adds shards to cut wait time while another removes jobs to cut cost, each undoing the other's work.

Questions people ask

How do I see billed minutes?

In the billing and usage pages of your GitHub organization or account settings, which break usage down by workflow and runner type.

Why is my run slow but steps are fast?

The time is probably queue time. Compare createdAt and startedAt of the run, and look at concurrency limits.

See the numbers

Look at the CI metrics of any public GitHub repo, no account needed.

Cilytics shows pass rate trend, red workflows, median and p90 run time, flaky and slow workflows for an org or a single public repo. It uses default-branch runs from the last 100 runs and an hourly snapshot.
