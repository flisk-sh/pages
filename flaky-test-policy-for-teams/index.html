A Flaky Test Policy for Teams: Owners, Budgets and Deadlines

A simple written policy that keeps flaky tests from piling up: who owns them, how many you tolerate, how long they live and when to delete.

A flaky test policy answers four questions: who owns a flake, how many you tolerate, how long it may live, and what happens at the deadline. Written down and checked weekly, it stops flakes from accumulating until nobody trusts CI.

Give every flake an owner

A flake without an owner is never fixed. Assign it to the team that owns the code under test, not to whoever hit it. Open one issue per flaky test with a standard title and label, such as flaky, and link the failing runs. Paste the error text and the failure rate you measured.

Set a budget

Pick a number your team can hold. For example: no more than five quarantined tests, and the default branch pass rate for the main workflow stays above an agreed target. When the budget is full, new feature work in that area waits for fixes. Choose your own numbers. They matter less than having them and noticing when they are broken.

Set deadlines and the delete rule

  • Day 0: the test is tagged and moved to quarantine.
  • Day 14: fix it, or write why it cannot be fixed.
  • Day 30: delete it and cover the behavior with a smaller test.

Deleting is a legitimate outcome. A test that fails randomly and cannot be fixed gives negative value: it costs time and teaches people to ignore red.

Make it visible

Put the numbers where the team looks: the run summary, a weekly message, or a dashboard. Count quarantined tests, retries that passed and flaky workflows. A public dashboard like Cilytics can show flaky workflows for any public repo with ?repo=owner/name, though it only sees default-branch runs and hourly snapshots, so your own test-level counts are still needed.

A policy you can copy

Here is a short version to paste in your contributing guide. A flaky test gets an issue within one working day. It is quarantined within two. The owning team fixes or deletes it within 30 days. The quarantine list never exceeds a fixed size. Retries are allowed in CI, but retried tests are listed in the run summary. The weekly review is ten minutes and has a named person who runs it. Adjust the numbers to your team, but keep the shape: owner, cap, deadline, review.

Review weekly, in ten minutes

Open the list. For each item ask: still flaky, who has it, is the deadline close. Close what is fixed. Escalate what is stuck. Then check the numbers in CI metrics that matter to see whether the trend is improving. Write the policy in your contributing guide, so new people see it on day one.

Questions people ask

Who should fix flaky tests, the author or the owner?

The owning team of the code under test. The person who first noticed it should file the issue with evidence, not necessarily fix it.

What flake rate is acceptable?

It is a team choice. Many teams aim for zero on the required checks and a small capped list in quarantine.

See your flaky workflows

Check which of your workflows flip between pass and fail.

Cilytics is a public CI health dashboard for GitHub. Open any public repo and it lists workflows that flipped between pass and fail at least 3 times in the last 100 runs. No account needed.
